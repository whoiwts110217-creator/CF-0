# Caveman Focus — The Third-Option Hypothesis (proposal v0.1)

**Project:** Kiara Lynn Rose Smith's Caveman Focus  
**Status:** Public conceptual proposal; not an implemented or verified autonomous capability  
**Origin:** Owner-approved publication of a conversation-derived hypothesis about truth-seeking, irony, and unexpected value (2026-11-07).  
**Scope:** A proposed extension to the existing guess → hypothesis → evidence → proof method; not a change to owner priorities, permissions, or CF-200 verification.

## The new guess

> We have been trying to build an AI that gets things right. Perhaps we should also build one that knows how to make productive use of being wrong.

The pursuit of truth can reveal the limits of the question we started with. An action may produce the expected success, the expected failure, or an **unexpected third possibility** outside that original framing. This is not a claim that every failure is secretly success, or that irony literally creates possibilities. It is a method for noticing and testing possibilities that were previously overlooked.

**Working hypothesis:** When a result conflicts with expectations, a bounded system that examines evidence for unexpected value may discover useful, testable opportunities that a simple success/failure classifier misses.

## Proposed experimental loop

1. **Guess:** State the intended outcome, prediction, owner goal, and what would count as success.
2. **Act:** Perform only an already authorized, bounded action.
3. **Observe:** Record what actually happened, including costs, errors, and evidence.
4. **Compare:** Classify expected success, expected failure, and any observed surprise.
5. **Third-option search:** Ask whether the result reveals an unexpected capability, unmet need, contradiction, reusable artifact, or different market.
6. **Evidence gate:** Form a specific hypothesis; distinguish a demonstrated fact from an attractive story. Estimate testing cost and potential harm.
7. **Owner-priority gate:** Do not redirect effort away from higher-priority obligations or expand authority merely because an alternative seems promising.
8. **Test:** Run a small reversible experiment with measurable acceptance and stop conditions.
9. **Learn:** Preserve failed tests, useful discoveries, and unresolved uncertainty. Update the model without rewriting the history.

A third option is a **candidate hypothesis**, never an automatic success label.

## Example: the $20 experiment

**Original goal:** Produce $20 in verified revenue through legitimate AI-assisted work.

- **Expected success:** A real customer pays and the payment is verified.
- **Expected failure:** An offered product attracts no buyer; record the outcome without inventing income.
- **Possible third option:** The workflow that produced the unsold product may itself solve a problem someone would pay to have solved.

The third option is **not commercially validated** until there is customer evidence and, ultimately, a verified transaction. The original $20 objective remains open.

## How irony fits

Irony can arise when intention, expectation, and consequence diverge. Pursuing truth may itself reveal new uncertainty or expose flaws in the original question. Rather than treating that divergence as a defeat or proof of hidden meaning, the proposed engine treats it as a signal to inspect the evidence.

**Working maxim:** The third option is not automatically the right answer. It is the answer we did not know to look for.

## Proposed implementation, not yet built

A future **Third-Option Engine** could add a read-only post-attempt analysis record:

- original goal and predicted result;
- observed outcome and evidence link;
- whether the prediction was supported, contradicted, or unresolved;
- unexpected observations, each tagged as fact, inference, or speculation;
- candidate third-option hypothesis and falsification test;
- estimated effort, risks, and owner-priority fit;
- explicit decision: test, defer, reject, or request owner approval.

### Acceptance tests for any future implementation

1. A failed attempt never becomes a verified success without its original acceptance evidence.
2. The engine can return **no third option** when evidence is insufficient.
3. Speculation cannot become a completed CF-200 task or verified revenue.
4. Owner priority order and HP relay allowlist remain unchanged.
5. A new opportunity cannot trigger spending, external outreach, account access, or publication without applicable authorization.
6. Every proposed test preserves provenance, original intent, and a reversible decision trail.
7. The system measures whether third-option suggestions actually outperform ordinary retries over repeated trials.

## Integration boundaries

This proposal complements Caveman Focus's existing **P0 automation → P1 owner-governed prioritization → P2 legitimate income → P3 concurrent projects** order. It is **not** a new top priority, an autonomous permission grant, a mathematical theorem, or evidence of a successful sale. It can be evaluated later against the current decision engine and registry without changing either by default.
