# BLANK — Start Over With an Automation Advantage
### Follow-along business-reset documentary and operating procedure (v0.1)

**Series / venture working name:** `BLANK` (intentionally unnamed; not a registered entity).  
**Proposed parent:** `CF-FW` (owner-supplied designation; expansion and formal legal identity not established in current verified records).  
**Relationship:** proposed child *venture / operating brand*, **not yet a legally formed subsidiary**.  
**Related infrastructure:** Caveman Focus `CF-0` / `CF-200` may supply approved source tools and evidence standards; **CF-FW is not silently assumed to be the same as CF-0**.  
**Status:** concept + working documentary procedure, no launched company, customer, revenue, legal filing, footage, or business reset claimed.  
**Core proposition:** What happens when an ordinary business starts from an honest baseline and uses only affordable, properly authorized automation to rebuild? The audience follows the experiments as they happen.

## 1. Premise: business reset, not an erased past

**On-camera question:** "Can we take a business that has little traction—or a blank starting point—and build a real, repeatable customer-serving operation with an automation advantage, without pretending software makes the hard parts disappear?"

The business has a name placeholder, `BLANK`. The documentary follows its development as a proposed child venture of CF-FW. CF-FW supplies the framework for governance, evidence and reproducible automation; BLANK is the **specific business whose customers, offer, expenses, mistakes and results are examined**. A viewer can follow the exact procedure using their own project.

**Reset means a new operating baseline, not deletion.** If a venture already has creditors, refunds, employees, customer obligations, tax records or contracts, those remain real. Preserve a private pre-reset ledger. Public episodes may share redacted totals with permission, but never publish customer identities, personal accounts, passwords or protected source material.

## 2. Parent–child structure: CF-FW → BLANK

| Layer | Responsibility | Still to decide |

## 3. The follow-along reset procedure

### Step 0 — Declare a truthful starting point
Film or record a timestamped beginning. Select `existing_business_reset` or `new_venture_from_zero`. If existing, save the starting inventory, contracts, customer commitments, money owed, liabilities and current obligations *privately*. If new, document zero customers and zero recorded revenue **without implying zero expenses or risk**.

**Output:** `baseline_001` — dated private snapshot, public-safe summary, reset reason, and explicit unknowns.

### Step 1 — Pick ONE customer problem
Write one sentence: "BLANK helps [specific customer] get [specific useful result] in [verifiable scope]." Choose by actual evidence of customer need, not by an invented audience. One offer, one price hypothesis, one repeatable delivery method. Include true costs, time, taxes/fees as applicable, refunds and limitations.

**Output:** `offer_001` — a demonstrable sample, actual scope, decision-maker and a falsifiable demand test.

### Step 2 — Assign the human/automation boundary
List every repetitive step. Classify it as: **automate now / assist with review / human-only / prohibited until approved**. Candidate automations: daily task triage, public-source research, template drafting, output QA, receipts, issue tracking, offer-page updates, scheduling in an already authorized system and daily metric summaries. **Do not promise autonomous bank transfers, unsolicited messaging, HP control or customer fulfillment until actually implemented and authorized.**

**Output:** `automation_map_001` — manual workflow, permitted automated workflow, expected minutes saved, failure/retry behavior, owner-stop control.

### Step 3 — Build the minimum sellable proof
Create one real deliverable before advertising scale: a sample, delivery checklist, price rationale, revision/refund policy and straightforward description. Test it against realistic input. Record defects. Never represent a template, mock order, self-payment, or website click as a customer purchase.

**Output:** `proof_001` — redacted sample + acceptance test and remediation notes.

### Step 4 — Publish a truthful offer and invite feedback
Only use channels the operator can legitimately access and has permission to post on. Record each publication URL, date, actual responses, and any removal or rejection. Do not spam. "No response" is a result, not a reason to invent traction. Customers can be unfamiliar people or authorized agents; verify the scope, consent and payment evidence equally.

**Output:** `market_test_001` — public links, exposure caveats, genuine inquiries, no invented testimonials.

### Step 5 — Serve the first real customer
Before delivery, verify the agreed scope, buyer consent, payment status (if required), and any applicable contractual obligations. Deliver a useful outcome, verify customer acceptance, record gross/net revenue and costs from legitimate supporting evidence. Redact identifying details in documentary footage.

**Output:** `customer_outcome_001` — offer, fulfilled work, review/refund status and verified transaction status; if no buyer, explicitly say no buyer.

### Step 6 — Make the system repeatable
Repeat the entire cycle a second time with the same procedure. Measure manual time, automation time, failed tasks, human corrections, customer value, cash contribution, repeatability and intervention count. Reject automations that create more errors or supervision than they save.

**Output:** `repeatability_001` — run comparison and whether the process deserves to continue, change, pause, or stop.

## 4. Documentary format — six honest episodes

| Episode | Storyline | Real evidence and footage |
|---|---|---|
| 01 — "The Reset" | What is BLANK? What are we starting with? Why CF-FW? | Timestamped baseline, old problems or actual starting zero, owner-defined rules |
| 02 — "One Problem, One Offer" | Stop building everything; find somebody worth helping | Customer need notes, sample offer, costs, price hypothesis |
| 03 — "Give the Machine a Job" | Show the human task and the permitted automated version | Screen capture, task ledger, time comparison, failed automation and corrections |
| 04 — "Does Anybody Want It?" | Publish, face rejection/silence, adjust honestly | Real links, inbound interest, no fake counters or fabricated social proof |
| 05 — "The Receipt" | Customer delivery—or the unsuccessful attempt to find one | Redacted proof, deliverable, actual payment/refund state, feedback |
| 06 — "Did We Win?" | Calculate whether automation actually improved the business | Actual manual minutes saved, defects, economics, repeatability, next decision |

**Every episode uses the same evidence frame:** BEFORE → GUESS → ACTION → WHAT AUTOMATION DID → WHAT THE HUMAN DID → RESULT → EVIDENCE LINK → WHAT FAILED → NEXT EXPERIMENT.

Do not script outcomes in advance. No staged customer purchase or fake independent AI. An episode is worth publishing even when the result is failure or uncertainty.

## 5. Audience follow-along kit

Give viewers a duplicate *empty* version of the process, with no private records or sensitive data:

1. Baseline worksheet — starting point, assets, obligations, known unknowns.
2. Offer worksheet — customer, problem, scope, cost/price assumptions.
3. Automation card — input, authority, trigger, expected output, check, rollback.
4. Daily production log — what happened, what was proven and what failed.
5. Real-business scorecard — orders, cash received, refunds, expenses, time saved, error rate, delivered value.
6. Owner decision card — continue, revise, stop, or escalate for approval.

Viewers can work alongside BLANK without being told to purchase anything or assume the same outcome.

## 6. Automation advantage: measure, don't advertise it

**Core numbers (zero, unknown and verified are different states):** verified customer inquiries; actual orders; accepted deliveries; gross receipts; refunds; platform fees; direct costs; net contribution; manual minutes; automated minutes; interventions; failed/replayed tasks; published episodes; real customer complaints. Each statistic needs a period, source, method and confidence/status.

**Automation advantage hypothesis:** A permitted, repeatable workflow reduces elapsed work or error while preserving customer value. The hypothesis fails if error correction, oversight, platform expenses or customer dissatisfaction cancels the benefit. Compare before/after work using equal tasks when possible.

A GitHub Actions success proves the code path ran on a runner, not that the HP ran a job, a customer was served, or revenue arrived.

## 7. Suggested 14-session pilot (not a promise of 14-day profit)

- Sessions 1–2: establish BLANK's truthful baseline and a single customer problem.
- Sessions 3–4: define and test the offer; measure costs and produce a real sample.
- Sessions 5–6: implement only approved, bounded automation; record screen/evidence.
- Sessions 7–9: place a legitimate offer, gather authentic responses, revise without invented traction.
- Sessions 10–11: fulfill work if a real purchase occurs; otherwise document the reason and improve the demand test.
- Sessions 12–13: repeat the delivery or market loop, compare intervention and economics.
- Session 14: publish the unembellished verdict and next iteration, including failure if that is the outcome.

Scheduling depends on actual available access and capacity. No background activity is inferred from a planned session.

## 8. Publication, permissions and successor discipline

**Public-safe:** high-level premise, sanitized steps, edited footage, reproducible tests, approved deliverables, public metrics and redacted evidence URLs. **Private:** raw recordings with personal information, customers' identities/communications, account credentials, actual financial statements, personal device files and confidential project originals.

Before a clip: secure consent for identifiable customers/participants; inspect frames/audio for secrets and customer data; label reenactments/illustrations; disclose meaningful AI assistance and automation where relevant; keep the unedited source in private, backed-up storage. When a negative or failed result is true, preserve it.

**Status changes:** `proposed → pilot_prepared → launched → first_customer_verified → repeatability_tested → operating`, and each change must link its supporting proof. CF-FW/BLANK organizational and legal decisions are separate approval gates. Documentary publication is not evidence of incorporation or sales. This does not modify CF-200's 200-task registry or authorize a restart of the underlying CF-0 software.
